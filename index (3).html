Academic profile

About me

I am a doctoral researcher in economics and finance at the Doctoral School of The John Paul II Catholic University of Lublin, Poland.

My academic interests include international economics, international trade and exchange-rate policy, with a particular focus on the Chinese economy and China's trade relations with the United States and the European Union. I use applied econometrics to examine the effects of exchange-rate movements on trade balances, including asymmetric responses and the J-curve hypothesis. I am also interested in the cultural foundations of Chinese society.

Current research

Exchange rates and the trade balance: China–United States

Empirical analysis of bilateral trade-balance adjustment using aggregate and sector-level trade data, including nonlinear dynamic specifications.

Exchange rates and the trade balance: European Union–China

Ongoing work on the EU–China trade relationship, with a focus on model specification, variable construction and time-series diagnostics.

J-curve research: evidence and methods

A review of empirical approaches to the J-curve hypothesis, including methodological choices and the interpretation of existing findings.
